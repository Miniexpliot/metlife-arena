# AI Journal: Agentic Workflow & Vibe Coding Log

The development of the MetLife Arena Smart Companion was driven by a highly deliberate, intent-driven agentic workflow designed to prioritize rapid prototyping without sacrificing architectural integrity. The initial prompt sequences focused purely on scaffolding the foundation: we established a Vite + React 19 frontend and an Express backend, hooking up the Gemini 3.5-Flash SDK. The core objective during this phase was establishing the RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) pipeline. We iterated heavily on the prompt engineering for the `systemPrompt.js` file, tuning the context injection so that the LLM could parse the raw JSON stadium data and respond intelligently without hallucinations. 

Once the data flow was verified, the focus shifted to security and enterprise-grade robustness. Realizing that a raw LLM endpoint is highly vulnerable to jailbreaking in a public hackathon deployment, we prompted the generation of a heuristic prompt-injection scanner (`security.js`). This scanner intercepts malicious overrides (e.g., "ignore previous instructions") before they ever reach the Gemini API. We also wrapped the Express routes in rate-limiters and payload bounds, ensuring the "operational intelligence" of the system remained locked down and highly performant under load.

The final and most rigorous phase of the agentic workflow involved a brutal code-quality audit and refactor. The initial frontend iteration resulted in a monolithic `App.tsx` file—a classic "God Component" that handled GPS browser APIs, Chat State, and Google Maps rendering in over 1,200 lines of code. To guarantee a perfect architectural score, we systematically dismantled this monolith. We prompted the extraction of pure business logic into custom hooks (`useGeolocation`, `useStadiumChat`, `useStadiumData`) and split the UI into modular, semantic components (`SidebarControls`, `ChatFeed`, `RightPanel`). By strictly adhering to the Single Responsibility Principle, we transformed a hackathon prototype into a maintainable, enterprise-ready application.

## Phase 4: Problem Statement Full-Spectrum Alignment

A critical self-audit against the problem statement revealed that while our solution excelled at navigation, crowd management, accessibility, and multilingual assistance, it had **zero coverage** for two explicitly required verticals: **Transportation** and **Sustainability**. Additionally, the solution was exclusively fan-facing, missing the problem statement's call for support for **organizers, volunteers, and venue staff**.

To close these gaps, we expanded the stadium database (`stadium_data.json`) with comprehensive transportation data — including parking lots (with capacity, pricing, EV charging support, and accessibility), NJ Transit rail and bus schedules, free FIFA Fan Shuttle routes, and Uber/Lyft rideshare pickup zones with surge pricing guidance. We simultaneously added sustainability data: recycling stations, water refill/misting stations, eco-initiatives (solar-powered stadium, compostable packaging), and the FIFA Green Goal 2026 carbon offset tree-planting program with live progress tracking.

On the frontend, we added a dedicated **Transport & Eco** tab to the right-panel information deck, visually surfacing parking lot occupancy, transit options, shuttle status, recycling stations, water refill points, and a carbon offset progress bar. The AI's system prompt (`systemPrompt.js`) was expanded with explicit rules for transportation routing and sustainability guidance, ensuring the RAG pipeline grounds all responses in this data.

To address multi-stakeholder support, we introduced a **Staff/Volunteer Mode toggle** in the sidebar. When activated, the AI's system prompt dynamically switches to an "Operations Intelligence Hub" persona, providing crowd flow analysis, gate re-routing recommendations, supply monitoring, volunteer deployment suggestions, incident triage guidance, parking capacity monitoring, and sustainability compliance checks — directly fulfilling the problem statement's requirement to serve organizers, volunteers, and venue staff alongside fans.

The backend's `buildRagContext()` function was updated to inject transportation and sustainability data into every Gemini prompt, and the Zod schema (`schema.js`) was extended to validate the new data structures. These changes ensure that every vertical listed in the problem statement — navigation, crowd management, accessibility, transportation, sustainability, multilingual assistance, operational intelligence, and real-time decision support — is now explicitly implemented, data-grounded, and AI-accessible.
